Cricket is a globally popular bat-and-ball sport played between two teams of eleven players on a circular or oval-shaped field. At the center of the field is a 22-yard rectangular pitch with a set of three wooden stumps—called a wicket—at each end. The primary objective is for the batting team to score as many runs as possible while the bowling and fielding team attempts to restrict the scoring and dismiss the batters.

The game operates in phases called "innings," where one team bats and the other fields. A team scores runs by hitting the ball bowled at them and running between the wickets, or by hitting the ball to or over the boundary line for automatic runs (four or six). The fielding team can dismiss batters in several ways, including bowling them out (hitting the stumps with the ball), catching a hit ball before it touches the ground, or running them out before they cross the crease.

Cricket is played in three primary international formats, each offering a different pace and strategy:

Test Cricket: The traditional and longest format, played over five days with each team batting twice. It is considered the ultimate test of a player's endurance, technique, and strategic thinking.

One Day International (ODI): A limited-overs format where each team faces 50 overs (an over consists of six legal deliveries). It balances traditional technique with faster scoring and typically lasts about eight hours.

Twenty20 (T20): The shortest and most explosive format, with each team facing just 20 overs. Matches last about three hours and prioritize aggressive batting, innovative shot-making, and high-energy fielding.

Originating in southeast England in the late 16th century, cricket has grown into one of the world's most-watched sports, governed globally by the International Cricket Council (ICC). It enjoys immense cultural significance and a massive fanbase, particularly in South Asia, Australia, the United Kingdom, and Southern Africa, with major tournaments like the ICC Cricket World Cup drawing billions of viewers.
